Our Research

Our vision is to develop technologies that enable the miniaturization of atom-based systems into platforms that are scalable, compact, energy-efficient, and robust to unavoidable external perturbations. By engineering reliable, high-performance quantum systems, we aim to access previously unexplored regimes and address pressing challenges in the field, enabling both fundamental scientific discovery and technological advances in quantum computing and precision metrology.

Our work is interdisciplinary, drawing on the fundamental physics of atom–light interactions while leveraging integrated photonics and microelectromechanical systems (MEMS). In our research, we use micro- and nanofabrication techniques for device fabrication and metrology, state-of-the-art laser and electronic control systems, ultra-high-vacuum and cryogenic technologies, packaging methods for integrated microsystems, and numerical as well as theoretical approaches.

The technologies we develop have applications that extend beyond quantum systems to, e.g. visible and ultra-violet integrated photonics, co-packaged optics, integrated optical sources and amplifiers.

Selected research themes

Integrated optical addressing and control for Trapped Ions and Cold Atoms

The aim within this theme is to endow microfabricated surface traps with photonic integration. With continuous development in this domain, we aim to replicate the performance of free-space-based optical light delivery, albeit in a compact footprint that is amenable to scalability, paving the way beyond single-zone architectures towards multiple trapping zones, a prerequisite for a large-scale quantum platform.

Integrated Atomic Microsystems

Chip-scale atomic clocks are a successful example of miniaturized atomic systems. We aim to contribute towards similar systems that extend beyond hot atoms to cold atom ensembles, and finally to individually controlled atoms/ions. This entails the integration of multiple subcomponents required in such an assembly and making them power-efficient, compact, and robust from thermal and acoustic aspects.

    Integrated light sources and modulators for atom-light interaction

    In this line of research, we aim to develop efficient coherent light sources and amplifiers required to address atomic transitions with the requisite linewidth and frequency referencing. We aim to develop photonic modulators required for laser pulse shaping that is needed for performing quantum operations involving atomic transitions.
